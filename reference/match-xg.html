Read shot volume and quality together

Shots: number of included attempts.

Total xG: sum of their scoring probabilities.

xG per shot: total xG divided by shots; undefined if there are no shots.

Use matching inclusion rules and one model for comparisons. Label non-penalty totals as npxG. Count each shot once, even when several passes preceded it. Own goals and penalties can make shot-goal totals differ from the final score.

Totals may exceed 1: they are expected goal counts, not win probabilities. A higher total is not a guaranteed result. A single goals-minus-xG gap does not prove finishing ability. Summing expectations requires no independence assumption; converting the probabilities into a score distribution requires additional modelling.

Cumulative xG: running sum in shot order. A shot adds its probability whether scored or missed; a goal marker adds no extra xG. Separate periods to retain added time correctly. Flat stretches mean no included shots, not no possession.

Shot map: locations show where attempts started. Normalize direction for comparison and keep one marker-area scale across panels. Mean distance summarizes the group; individual shots can differ. Locations alone do not reveal the buildup or tactical cause. Worked shot-map lesson.

Volume × quality: total xG = shot count × average xG per shot. Between two groups, multiply retained count and quality proportions to obtain the retained total proportion. Percentage reductions do not add. Zero-shot groups have undefined mean quality; a zero reference gives an undefined ratio. These are descriptive comparisons, not tactical causes. Worked example.

Body part: a reporting tag does not become a model feature until the formula uses it. Left/right contact foot does not establish preferred/weak-foot status. Small group conversion rates do not establish general effectiveness. Body-part lesson.

Trace an assisting pass: follow shot.key_pass_id and verify pass.assisted_shot_id points back. Check team, period, possession and event order. Missing links are unresolved. Shot type alone does not identify the preceding attacking phase; inspect pass type and play pattern. Header-link lesson.

Corner rates: define exposure first. All corner pass events include incomplete deliveries. Recorded direct shot links / deliveries measures explicit assisting links, not all later corner-phase chances. Keep unknown links distinct from known outcomes, and distinguish deliveries from corners awarded. Corner-denominator lesson.

Corner-phase proxy: specify possession, time and restart/period/goal boundaries before counting. Corners with any shot and total associated shots are different quantities. Keep assignments unique; phase association does not establish causation. Worked phase assignment.
