Football analyst training · Recruitment phase · Lesson 69

Turn the analysis into a useful brief.

Data: FIFA Women's World Cup, 2019 — StatsBomb competition 72, season 30.
Read: Soccermatics Lesson 3 — Statistical scouting → Modesty.

A coach or scout needs a clear finding, the limits of that finding, and a useful next action.

The completed assignment

We created a styled recruitment brief and a GitHub-readable version, generated from the existing evidence.

  1. Finding: Bronze recorded more eligible completions per 90 in her selected matches: 45.57 versus Majri's 30.70.
  2. Context: Bronze's starts were at right back; Majri's were at left back. Team possession, opponents and pass difficulty are not adjusted for.
  3. Next action: review four specific passes, with recorded match clocks and source IDs, then check whether patterns recur.
Make a claim the evidence supports, name its limits, and explain what would help decide next.

What we did not observe

We have not watched footage of those passes. The review queue deliberately illustrates the fixed and near-goal definitions; it is not a representative performance sample. We do not label marking, pressure or decision quality from the pass arrows alone.

These historical examples do not establish who should be signed today. They demonstrate how to prepare information for further assessment. This follows the course's “Modesty” section: use analysis to help others make informed judgments.

Practice: which claim can we support—“Bronze is the better signing” or “Bronze had higher selected completed-pass activity per 90”?

The second. We measured selected passing activity. A signing decision needs wider evidence about role, performance, fit and current circumstances.
