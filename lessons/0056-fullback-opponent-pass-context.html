Time is not the only denominator.

Data: FIFA Women's World Cup, 2019 — StatsBomb competition 72, season 30.
Read: Soccermatics Lesson 3 — Statistical scouting → Normalisation.

We already counted pressures per 90 minutes. But two players can play the same time while facing different amounts of opponent passing. We now calculate a second view of their activity.

The completed assignment

Bronze: 102 pressures ÷ 2,034 opponent passes ×100 = 5.01
Majri:   61 pressures ÷ 1,248 opponent passes ×100 = 4.89

Here “opponent passes” means eligible attempts, including failed attempts. We exclude restart deliveries, unknown/injury-clearance outcomes and passes by a team other than the recorded possession team. Only passes during each player's selected registered windows count. We pool all selected matches before dividing.

Per 90: Bronze 13.28, Majri 10.89.
Per 100 eligible opponent attempts: Bronze 5.01, Majri 4.89.
The difference looks smaller with this denominator. The recorded actions have not changed.

What you should understand

Changing the denominator changes the question. Per 90 asks about activity in equal time. Per 100 opponent attempts asks about activity relative to opponent passing volume. This second measure is a rough exposure proxy: it does not directly measure possession time or individual pressing opportunities.

It does not mean Bronze pressured exactly five of every 100 passes. Pressures and passes are separate events. A pressure can happen during a carry, and several players can pressure during one passage. This ratio is not a success percentage and can exceed 100.

Quick application

Player A records 10 pressures against 100 opponent attempts. Player B records 10 against 200. Who has more pressure activity relative to this exposure?

Check the reasoning

A: 10/100 ×100 =10. B: 10/200 ×100 =5. Equal pressure counts, different opponent passing volume. This alone cannot tell us who defended better.
