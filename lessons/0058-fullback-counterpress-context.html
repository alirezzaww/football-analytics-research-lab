What happens just after losing the ball?

Data: FIFA Women's World Cup, 2019 — StatsBomb competition 72, season 30.
Read: Soccermatics Lesson 3 — Statistical scouting → Context: what to measure.
Definition: StatsBomb event specification → Pressure → counterpress.

Counterpressing means applying pressure soon after your team loses the ball during play. In the provider's definition, the pressing action is within five seconds of an open-play turnover.

A simple imagined example

12:00   England lose the ball during play.
12:03   Bronze pressures the opponent.

This illustrates counterpressing. It does not tell us who gets the ball next. This timeline is a teaching example, not an observation from our matches.

The completed assignment

Our code reads the provider's counterpress flag on the same Pressure events. We do not guess the lost-ball instant or create a new detection system.

Bronze: 20 flagged pressures ÷ 102 total ×100 = 19.6%
Majri:  17 flagged pressures ÷  61 total ×100 = 27.9%

We calculated all 35 players: 353 of the 2,034 selected Pressure events are flagged. Original minutes, fixtures and total pressure counts are unchanged. Missing map locations do not remove an event.

Bronze had more flagged counterpressures: 20 versus 17.
Majri had a larger share of pressures flagged: 27.9% versus 19.6%.

Three different questions

  • Pressure location: where did she press?
  • Counterpressing: was the pressure flagged as soon after a loss?
  • Pressure regain: did her team recover the ball shortly after pressure? We have not calculated this outcome here.

Counterpressing can happen anywhere on the pitch. Bronze's 20 flagged events are selected using timing context, separately from the 20 opponent-third events in the previous lesson. Equal totals do not mean the same events.

How the data records it

event['type']['name'] == 'Pressure'
event.get('counterpress') is True

Only Boolean True counts as flagged. The specification normally provides the field only when true. We preserve absent, null and false states separately and call these events “not flagged.” They are not video-verified non-counterpresses.

Practice: a player applies pressure two seconds after losing possession, but the opponent keeps the ball. Can this still be counterpressing?

Yes. Counterpressing describes the pressure after losing possession. Winning the ball back is a separate outcome.

These are actions, not unique lost-ball episodes: several players or pressures may belong to one episode. More counterpressures does not prove better defending; a team may simply lose possession more often. No opportunity adjustment or success percentage is calculated.
