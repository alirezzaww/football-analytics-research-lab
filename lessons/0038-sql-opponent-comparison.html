Question: in each France match, what percentage of corners led to at least one shot under our existing 15-second phase rule?

One new idea: GROUP BY

Put Korea corners together, Norway corners together, and so on. Count within each group instead of mixing all five matches.

GROUP BY c.opponent

Against Korea: 13 corners, 6 with a shot. The rate is 6 ÷ 13 × 100 = 46.2%. Seven shots followed those corners, but we count the six corners with a shot in the numerator.

Keep the counting correct

First group shots by match ID and corner ID. This produces at most one shot-summary row per corner. LEFT JOIN that summary to all corners. Finally group those corner rows by opponent.

Summarize shots per corner → connect to every corner → summarize corners per opponent.

ORDER BY corner_shot_rate_pct DESC displays higher rates first. It changes display order, not the calculation.

Completed worked assignment

Run 56_sql_opponent_comparison.py in Spyder. The SQL query reproduces all five previously calculated match rates from the relational tables.

USA: 4/7 = 57.1%; Korea: 6/13 = 46.2%; Nigeria: 4/9 = 44.4%; Norway: 3/9 = 33.3%; Brazil: 2/7 = 28.6%.

These are small, single-match descriptions. They do not establish which opponent is worst at defending corners or which delivery tactic is best. Pool the counts for the overall result: 19/45 = 42.2%; do not average the five percentages.

Practice: USA has 4 positive corners and 4 shots. Korea has 6 positive corners and 7 shots. Which group had the higher corner-shot rate?

USA: 4/7 = 57.1%, versus Korea 6/13 = 46.2%. Korea produced more total shots, while a higher share of USA-match corners led to a shot. Different questions need different denominators.
