Which completed passes move the ball far forward?

Data: FIFA Women's World Cup, 2019 — StatsBomb competition 72, season 30.
Read: Soccermatics Lesson 3 — Statistical scouting → Context: what to measure.

Last time we counted passing activity. Now we add one detail: how far each completed pass advances along the pitch.

Our explicit rule

Forward gain = end_x − start_x
Count the completion when the gain is at least 20.

x=40 → x=65: gain 25 → counts
x=70 → x=75: gain  5 → does not count

These are units on the pitch's 0–120 length scale, not metres. In the normalized data, the attacking goal is at x=120. A long sideways pass can have no forward gain.

The completed assignment

  • Bronze: 34 qualifying completions → 4.43 per 90; 9.7% of her 350 eligible completions.
  • Majri: 18 → 3.21 per 90; 10.5% of her 172 eligible completions.

Bronze has more qualifying completions per 90 in this sample. Majri has a slightly larger qualifying share of her completed passes. Those are different denominators.

This adds direction to the profile. It does not measure every valuable pass or prove who is better.

Twenty is a transparent project choice, not a universal progressive-pass standard. A valuable short pass near goal can fail the rule. Defenders, pressure and starting-zone opportunities are not adjusted for. The maps also reflect their different sides and roles.

A real recorded example

One Bronze completion starts at x=10.4 and ends at x=32.5. Its gain is 22.1, so it counts. The full report preserves its match and event ID.

Practice: does a completed pass from x=110 to x=119 count under this rule?

No: its gain is nine. It can still be a valuable pass. The rule measures a specific kind of advancement, not overall pass value.
