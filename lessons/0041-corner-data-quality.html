Can we trust the records we join?

Before interpreting the corner-taker percentages, check that the records connect correctly.

A small error can change the result

Correct toy records:
Corner A → shot
Corner B → no shot
Rate: 1/2 = 50%

If A is accidentally included twice:
Corner A → shot
Corner A → shot
Corner B → no shot
Rate from these duplicated records: 2/3 = 66.7%

Nothing improved on the pitch. The dataset gained a duplicate. This is a toy example, not an error found in our real data. A corner repeated by a legitimate one-to-many shot join is a different situation: the original corner record should still occur only once.

The completed assignment

We checked all 45 corners, 21 phase shots and 45 taker records. Nine structural checks passed, including duplicate IDs, missing takers, unknown corner references and blank names.

A corner with no shot is valid. A corner missing its required taker record is a data problem for this player report.

The audit inspects temporary tables before loading the strict reporting tables. If it finds a problem, the player-report loader stops and names the failed check. We investigate rather than silently deleting records.

What passing means

The checked keys and relationships are consistent. Passing does not prove every real-world event was recorded correctly or make our tactical conclusions true.

Practice: a corner has a known taker but no matching phase shot. Should we remove it?

No. Keep it as a zero-shot corner. Removing it would shrink the denominator and inflate the shot-associated percentage.
