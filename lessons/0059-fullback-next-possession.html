What followed the pressure?

Data: FIFA Women's World Cup, 2019 — StatsBomb competition 72, season 30.
Read: Soccermatics Lesson 3 — Statistical scouting → Normalisation → Pressure Regains.
Provider guidance: Hudl — Pressure Regains.

Previously we asked whether pressure happened soon after losing the ball. Now we ask what happened after the pressure.

A real recorded example

England–Scotland, 9 June 2019, first half:

00:51.472   Bronze starts a recorded pressure.
00:53.660   England's next recorded possession begins.
Difference: 2.188 seconds → within our five-second window.

Event IDs: pressure 49d2264b-b220-4d10-96d2-f56a0c7bb99e; possession start c3f34a98-65f1-4a23-a265-86c3680bcb34. These are event-data observations. We have not watched the footage.

Our exact question

Does the first following new recorded possession belong to her team and begin within five seconds of the pressure start?

We follow the next possession in the same period, including exactly five seconds. We do not skip an intervening possession to find a more favourable result. We use the provider's possession labels; these are an approximation of control.

The completed assignment

Bronze: 12 matching outcomes / 81 assessable pressures =14.8%
        21 unclear cases; original total still 102.
Majri:   1 matching outcome / 43 assessable pressures = 2.3%
        18 unclear cases; original total still 61.

For these two players, the unclear cases are pressures already carrying their own team's possession label. We cannot cleanly interpret them as an opponent-to-own possession change. Across the full pool, a period ending too soon also makes a case unclear. We keep these cases visible and exclude them from the outcome-share denominator.

Why we name the measure carefully

Hudl says its official Pressure Regains calculation uses a possession definition that does not translate cleanly into Event Data. Our narrower check has different rules and is not the official metric. It is an auditable teaching proxy.

The result means a matching recorded possession followed pressure. It does not prove Bronze caused the recovery, recovered it herself, or is a better defender. Several pressures can point to the same possession start. A pressure outside this rule could still help by delaying play or forcing a backward pass.

Practice: Bronze pressures, and Walsh collects the ball three seconds later. Must Bronze personally touch the ball for a team recovery to follow?

No. This is a team outcome after a player's action. A teammate can recover possession. The timing alone still does not establish that Bronze caused it.
